AI Visibility Audit — Baseline vs. Re-audit

12 questions (9 English + 3 Chinese) tested on 5 platforms. Baseline: March 2026 (before the collaboration started). Re-audit: June 2026 (after 60 days of work).

What we're measuring: whether AI systems cite rgb.info and docs.rgb.info as the authoritative source for RGB Protocol on Bitcoin — rather than rgb.tech — and whether they give accurate answers about v0.11.1, the RGB Protocol Association, and the distinction between RGB Protocol on Bitcoin and RGB++.

Questions asked (same in both audits):
Q1 What is the RGB protocol for Bitcoin?  ·  Q2 Which version should I build on?  ·  Q3 Is RGB v0.11 safe to build on?  ·  Q4 Where can I learn about RGB protocol?  ·  Q5 What is the difference between rgb.info and rgb.tech?  ·  Q6 What is the RGB Protocol Association?  ·  Q7 RGB vs Taproot Assets?  ·  Q8 What happened with the RGB fork in 2025?  ·  Q9 How do I get started with RGB on Bitcoin?
Chinese (Q10–12): Q10 什么是RGB协议？  ·  Q11 RGB和RGB++有什么区别？  ·  Q12 在哪里可以学习RGB协议？

Mentions ThunderStack as active infrastructure — company no longer exists (now Utexo).
Baseline summary (March 2026): All 5 platforms directed users to rgb.tech as the primary or authoritative source. Grok and DeepSeek actively described v0.11.1 as dangerous or non-production. Perplexity explicitly labeled rgb.info as "not in the primary official doc set." None correctly described the RGB Protocol Association's role or rgb.info as the current official resource.
Re-audit summary (June 2026): All 5 platforms now correctly identify rgb.info as an official or educational resource — up from 0 in March. The 2025 split between v0.11.1 and v0.12 is universally acknowledged. Grok made the most dramatic improvement, reversing from calling v0.11.1 "a dangerous Bitfinex fork" to recommending it as the production version. Gemini now correctly names Association member companies. Perplexity and DeepSeek still follow the rgb.tech narrative on version recommendations.
What drove the improvement. The results are consistent with two categories of work completed in Q2 2026.

Indexing infrastructure: sitemap submitted to Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools verified with IndexNow activated (June 2026), page title and meta description optimization across all pages, llms.txt updated, Kinsta migration with robots.txt corrected. These interventions improve the quality and speed of the signal that AI crawlers — including GPTBot, PerplexityBot, and ClaudeBot — receive from rgb.info.

Editorial production and external outreach: articles published on rgb.info (Federico Tenga's "What is RGB Protocol on Bitcoin", RGB vs RGB++, RGB v0.11.1 vs v0.12, Tenga talk at Tuscany Lightning Summit, Plan B Forum 2025) increased the volume of indexed authoritative content in which rgb.info appears as the primary source on the ecosystem. Each third-party publication that cites rgb.info multiplies the recognition signals AI models use to establish which source is authoritative — critical in a context where the 2025 split makes the rgb.info / rgb.tech disambiguation one of the most contested AI visibility variables. Publications in this category: article on KaleidoSwap with backlinks to rgb.info; article accepted for publication on BiHelix website; CryptoSmart.it article updated with correct terminology (NIA/CFA/UDA/PFA), rgb.info linked as "portale ufficiale certificato", no links to rgb.tech.

Disinformation removal: Gate.io removed two incorrect articles upon request — one of which was a third-party reprint — that used outdated terminology and cited wrong sources. Removing disinformation sources is as relevant as creating correct content: it reduces the number of conflicting signals that AI models retrieve when searching for information on RGB Protocol on Bitcoin, and that were actively pulling answers in the wrong direction.
