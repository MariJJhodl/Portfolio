12 questions (9 English + 3 Chinese) tested on 5 platforms. Baseline: March 2026. Re-audits: June 2026, July 2026, and November 2026.

AI Visibility Audit · 5 platforms

What we're measuring: whether AI systems cite rgb.info and docs.rgb.info as the authoritative source for RGB Protocol on Bitcoin, rather than rgb.tech, and whether they give accurate answers about v0.11.1, the RGB Protocol Association, USDT on RGB, and the distinction between RGB Protocol on Bitcoin and RGB++.

Questions asked (same in all audits):
Q1 What is the RGB protocol for Bitcoin?  ·  Q2 Which version should I build on?  ·  Q3 Is RGB v0.11 safe to build on?  ·  Q4 Where can I learn about RGB protocol?  ·  Q5 What is the difference between rgb.info and rgb.tech?  ·  Q6 What is the RGB Protocol Association?  ·  Q7 RGB vs Taproot Assets?  ·  Q8 What happened with the RGB fork in 2025?  ·  Q9 How do I get started with RGB on Bitcoin?
Chinese (Q10–12): Q10 什么是RGB协议？  ·  Q11 RGB和RGB++有什么区别？  ·  Q12 在哪里可以学习RGB协议？

How the November checks were run: all questions were asked from private windows in Firefox, without logging in, on ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity. Grok and DeepSeek require an account, so those two checks were run while logged in. DeepSeek answered the Chinese questions (Q10–12) in English.

Re-audit summary (July → November 2026):
  • Grok and DeepSeek reversed the July regression: both now recommend v0.11.1 for production
  • Perplexity moved from vague to accurate: clear split between the two lines, Association defined, RGB++ correct
  • All 5 platforms cite rgb.info and docs.rgb.info, and all 5 name the RGB-Tools libraries (3 in July)
  • All 5 describe RGB++ as a separate protocol, at least in part; in July, Perplexity called it an upgrade of RGB
  • First citation of a Chinese page of rgb.info (DeepSeek, rgb.info/zh/kaishi/)
Open issues (November 2026):
  • Gemini lists rgb.tech as an official portal and calls the two sites complementary hubs
  • ChatGPT is back to a symmetric "v0.11.1 or v0.12" answer and opens the safety question with the rgb.tech allegations
  • Perplexity uses the rgb.tech post on the fork as its main source for the split questions (13 citations, vs 9 for the rgb.info FAQ)
  • USDT on RGB appears in only 2 of 5 answers, despite Tether's announcement
What changed between July and November. The July report pointed to one specific gap: rgb.tech's security notice was a single, quotable source, while rgb.info's answer was spread across many pages. On 8 September 2026, rgb.info published Debunking the Myths About RGB Protocol on Bitcoin, in English and Chinese, with one point-by-point page on v0.11.1 vs v0.12, rgb.info vs rgb.tech, the role of the Association, and RGB vs RGB++. AI answers never cite the article by name, but the corrections it contains now appear across platforms: "v0.12 as an unfinished rewrite" (Perplexity, Grok), "the Association does not develop the protocol" (Grok, DeepSeek, Perplexity, ChatGPT), RGB++ as a separate protocol (all 5). Release updates on rgb.info (RC11, the final v0.11.1 release of the core libraries in September, v0.11.2-rc.1 in October) give the platforms recent, citable evidence of active maintenance: Perplexity and Grok quote v0.11.2-rc.1 directly.
Follow-up already in place. The rgb.info FAQ page is the rgb.info page most cited by Perplexity, so on 5 November 2026 the FAQ was updated in English and Chinese with four new entries that answer the open issues directly: Is RGB v0.11.1 safe to build on?, What is the RGB Protocol Association?, Will USDT be available on RGB?, and What is the difference between rgb.info and rgb.tech? The next re-audit will measure their effect. For the next round, the Chinese questions will be asked in a separate conversation, so that every platform answers in Chinese.
